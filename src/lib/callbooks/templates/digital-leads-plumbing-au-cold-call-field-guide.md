# Digital Leads Co — AU Plumbing Cold Call Field Guide

**Use with:** `digital-leads-plumbing-cold-call-book-au.ts`  
**Market:** Australia  
**Primary goal:** book a 10–15 minute audit / Growth Preview walkthrough, or agree one specific follow-up. Do not try to sell every package in the first call.

---

## 1. The call logic

**Evidence → relevance → discovery → recommendation → next step.**

1. Show you've done real work: one public Google Business Profile (GBP) observation and one website observation.
2. Explain why a customer might care, without claiming the business is losing jobs.
3. Ask what work, suburbs and enquiry channels matter to them.
4. Recommend only the package that matches the actual gap.
5. Ask for a small commitment: a short walkthrough, an agreed callback, or a properly scoped proposal.

The audit is the reason for the call, not the whole pitch. Keep the first explanation to roughly 20–30 seconds, then ask a question.

## 2. Before every call — fill this in

| Field | Prepare before dialling |
|---|---|
| Business / decision-maker | `[Business Name]`, `[Owner Name]`, role |
| Area / services | Verified suburbs and plumbing services shown publicly |
| GBP observation | One specific, visible fact; note where it appears |
| Website observation | One specific page / mobile / contact-flow fact |
| Evidence | Screenshot, exact URL, device or tool used |
| Customer relevance | Why the issue may make it harder to understand, trust or contact the business |
| Likely package | A hypothesis only; confirm in discovery |
| Next step | Two real meeting slots in the prospect's local time |

**Never infer:** private enquiry numbers, revenue, lead quality, actual GBP performance, Analytics or Search Console data. A public snapshot is not the full A$299 audit.

## 3. Core script

### Opening

> Hi, is that [Business Name]?
>
> Hi, it’s Ali calling from Digital Leads Co. Could I speak with the owner, or whoever looks after the website and Google Business Profile?

### Owner permission

> Hi [Name], it’s Ali calling from Digital Leads Co. We help plumbing businesses improve how their website and Google Business Profile bring in enquiries.
>
> I’ve had a quick look at yours and picked up [specific GBP observation] and [specific website observation]. Have you got 30 seconds for me to explain why I called?

### Audit hook

> The first thing I noticed on the Google profile was [GBP finding]. The second was on the website: [website finding].
>
> For a customer, that could mean [plain-English customer impact]. That’s an outside-in review, though — I can see the public-facing setup, not your actual calls or lead data.
>
> Can I ask, are you happy with the enquiries coming through Google and the website at the moment, or is improving that already on your radar?

### Discovery

> What sort of plumbing work would you most like to attract more of — general maintenance, emergency work, hot water, drainage, commercial, or something else?
>
> Which suburbs or service areas matter most to you?
>
> Where do most good enquiries come from at the moment — Google Business Profile, the website, referrals, paid ads, or repeat customers?

Only ask the questions that fit the conversation. Also qualify **capacity**: if more of the desired work came in, could the business take it on?

### Walkthrough close

> The easiest next step is a quick 10 to 15-minute walkthrough. I’ll show you the two audit findings, what a customer sees, and the options that make sense — including doing nothing if there’s no worthwhile change.
>
> Would [day/time option 1] or [day/time option 2] suit you better?

| Website is fine; wants maintenance and technical care | **Local Care** | A$149/month | Maintenance, security / backup / uptime checks, routine updates and up to 1 hour of minor content edits. Not SEO growth. |
| Site and local SEO foundation exist; wants steady monthly optimisation | **Local SEO Growth** | A$749/month | Up to 5 keywords, 3 competitors, 1 substantial page/content improvement per month, GBP maintenance and reporting. |
| Established multi-service / multi-suburb business wants broader monthly SEO | **Local SEO Expansion** | A$1,149/month | Up to 10 keywords, 5 competitors, up to 2 substantial content/page improvements per month and strategic review. |

**Important distinctions:** Website Launch is a website build. Local Visibility Foundation is a website plus local SEO setup. Local Care is maintenance. Local SEO Growth / Expansion are ongoing optimisation. A$299 Audit tells them what to prioritise; it does not include fixing it. Do not stack these by default.

When asked about price, say the published price plainly. Confirm GST treatment, third-party costs, payment terms, hosting/domain costs and exclusions in the written proposal — don't guess on the phone.

## 5. Phrase bank — natural Australian English

| Prefer | Avoid / use carefully |
|---|---|
| enquiries / quote requests | leads (can sound like agency jargon) |
| jobs / call-outs | conversions / funnel |
| suburbs / service areas | territories |
| Google Business Profile / Google profile | Google listing only, if it creates ambiguity |
| one-off investment | cheap / bargain / low-cost |
| monthly service / ongoing work | recurring fee (unless explaining a billing detail) |
| “Have you got 30 seconds?” | “This will only take a minute” |
| “Would it be worth a look?” | “You need a new website” |
| “That’s an outside-in review” | “Your SEO is bad” |
| “No worries” / “Fair enough” | forced “G’day mate” / overdone slang |

Use **investment** when talking about the business asset and its scope. Use **price** or **cost** when the prospect asks what it costs. Never hide the number behind sales language.

## 6. Objection response formula

**Acknowledge → clarify → respond to their actual reason → offer one next step.** Don't argue or stack rebuttals.

- **“We've already got a website.”**  
  “Absolutely. I'm not assuming you need a new one. Is the current site doing what you need — making services clear, showing the areas you cover, and making it easy to call or request a quote — or is there one part you'd improve?”

- **“The website works fine.”**  
  “Good — that's what you want. Are you looking to get more of a particular type of work, cover more suburbs, or mainly keep the current flow steady?”

- **“We've got an agency.”**  
  “Fair enough. If you're happy with them, I wouldn't suggest changing providers for the sake of it. Are they covering both the website and Google Business Profile, and are you happy with the reporting?”

- **“We're too busy.”**  
  “No worries — I don't want to catch you in the middle of a job. When's usually quieter for you?”

- **“Send me some info.”**  
  “Happy to. What's the best business email? Would you prefer I leave it there, or would one follow-up call after you've looked be alright?”

- **“That's expensive.”**  
  “Fair enough. Is it mainly above the budget you've set, or are you not yet sure the work would be worth the investment?”

- **“Someone else is cheaper.”**  
  “They may be a good fit. It’s worth comparing the scope side by side — pages, GBP optimisation, technical SEO, tracking, revisions, ownership and support — rather than just the headline price.”

- **“Can you guarantee top three / more leads?”**  
  “I wouldn't guarantee a particular Google position or number of calls. What we can commit to is the agreed work and the measurement plan we set together.”

- **“Not interested.”**  
  One gentle clarification at most. If they repeat the no, close. If they ask not to be contacted, stop immediately and suppress the number.

## 7. Call discipline

- Do not read the whole package menu unless asked about pricing.
- Do not describe the Free Growth Preview as a full audit.
- Do not recommend an SEO retainer if the site / local foundation needs to be rebuilt first.
- Do not recommend more enquiries to a business that has no capacity to take the work.
- Do not disparage the current agency or developer.
- Do not guarantee rankings, lead volume, jobs, ROI or revenue.
- Do not invent a case study, testimonial, integration, timeline or discount.
- Ask permission before sending a preview by email / WhatsApp / SMS; record the permission and agreed follow-up scope.
- A clear “no” is not an invitation to keep objection-handling.

## 8. Australia compliance reminder

Cold calling Australian numbers from outside Australia does not remove the Australian rules. Check the relevant Do Not Call Register requirements and any applicable consent before calling. Standard telemarketing hours are weekdays 9am–8pm, Saturdays 9am–5pm, and no Sundays or national public holidays. Identify yourself, your organisation and the reason for the call, display a return number, and end the call if asked. Mixed-use personal / business numbers need particular care.

For marketing email and SMS, obtain appropriate consent, identify the sender, provide contact details and make it easy to unsubscribe. Do not treat a number or email address being visible online as automatic consent to send a marketing message. Apply the current requirements to any messaging channel you use.
